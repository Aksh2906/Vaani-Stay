 │  ROUTE CARD  № 21                                          │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  DODRA KWAR — SHIMLA'S FORGOTTEN TRIBAL WORLD             │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  Days: 5  │  Region: Pabbar Valley, Shimla dist.          │
 │  Theme: Tribal villages + Chanshal Pass + wild meadows    │
 │  Season: MAY – OCTOBER only (pass snowed shut otherwise)  │
 │  Base: Rohru  │  Difficulty: ★★★☆☆  Moderate-Challenging  │
 │  Budget: ₹9,000–16,000/person                             │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  WAYPOINT  ①  SHIMLA → ROHRU
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Distance : 120 km from Shimla via Theog–Kotkhai–Hatkoti
  Road      : Paved, winding, scenic Pabbar Valley riverside
  Drive time: 4 hours
  Stop at   : Hatkoti Temple (10th-century Durga shrine,
               intricate woodwork, almost never crowded)
  Stop at   : Pabbar River viewpoint above Chirgaon —
               the river runs bright green through a narrow
               gorge and the apple trees crowd the banks

  Rohru town itself is unremarkable but functional: ATM,
  fuel, hot food, and a market. This is your last chance
  for all four before entering the valley proper.

  STAY: Basic hotel in Rohru  ₹700–1,200

  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  LOCAL SECRET #1                                    │
  │  The Mahasu Devta temple circuit around Rohru       │
  │  contains some of the finest carved wooden          │
  │  architecture in all of Himachal — none of it       │
  │  is in any guidebook. Ask locals for "Mahasu        │
  │  mandir" and follow their directions, not Google.   │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  WAYPOINT  ②  ROHRU → LAROT → CHANSHAL PASS
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Distance : 48 km Rohru to Larot, then 18 km to pass
  Road      : Paved to Larot, ROUGH dirt track beyond
              (requires high clearance SUV or motorbike)
  Drive time: 3–4 hours, don't rush
  Altitude  : Pass summit at 4,220m–4,520m
              (sources vary; it is very high, go slow)

  The road past Chanshal connects into the tribal regions
  of Dodra and Kwar — twin villages that live and breathe
  in a time of their own.  The meadows approaching
  the pass are extraordinary: wide grassy sweeps,
  wildflowers, and sweeping high-altitude landscapes with
  cool winds and open skies that feel far removed from
  busy town life. 

  At the summit: the snow-clad Chanshal range rises
  like a crown above the Pabbar Valley  and on clear
  days you see both the Shimla ranges behind you and
  the Rupin valley dropping ahead. Strong cold winds
  even in July — have your jacket within reach.

  STAY: Forest rest house at Larot  ₹400–600
        (book via HP Forest Dept, Rohru office)

  WAYPOINT  ③  CHANSHAL PASS → DODRA VILLAGE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Distance : 22 km descent from pass
  Road      : Extremely rough, seasonal, often muddy
  Drive time: 2 hours minimum

  Dodra Kwar are twin villages in the Rupin Valley accessed
  through Chanshal Pass, embodying Himalayan heritage with
  ancient wood-stone architecture and vibrant traditions. 
  These villages were completely cut off from the road
  network until very recently — the culture is intact in
  ways that roadside Himachal lost decades ago.

  Spend a full day in Dodra and Kwar — walk between
  them (4 km), talk to the weavers, see the ancient
  temple, eat whatever the homestay family cooks.

  STAY: Homestay in Dodra  ₹800–1,200 with meals

  WAYPOINT  ④  RUPIN RIVER WALK + REST DAY
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The Rupin river originates near here and the valley
  trail following it upstream (toward the famous Rupin
  Pass into Uttarakhand) is breathtaking even for a
  half-day walk. Rocky gorge, waterfalls, shepherd camps.

  This is where trekkers doing the full Rupin Pass
  trek enter from the Uttarakhand side. You can walk
  8–10 km upstream and return — no permit needed,
  no guide needed, no people.

  WAYPOINT  ⑤  RETURN TO ROHRU → SHIMLA
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Return the same way (or take the longer Uttarakhand
  border road if a second vehicle is available).
  Rohru overnight if late. Shimla next morning.

  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Rohru → Larot         ████████░░  Mostly paved
  Larot → Pass          █████░░░░░  Rough dirt/mud

  ON THE PLATE: Pabbar Valley apple, Pahari rajma,
  Madra (chickpea gravy), Til chutney, Rohru market
  jalebis, Larot dhaba chai, Village-brewed chhang.




 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  ROUTE CARD  № 22                                          │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  RUPIN PASS TREK — HIMACHAL INTO UTTARAKHAND              │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  Days: 8  │  Region: Sangla (Kinnaur) to Dhaula (UK)      │
 │  Theme: Cross-state high-pass trek                        │
 │  Season: MAY–JUNE, SEPTEMBER–OCTOBER                      │
 │  Start: Sangla (Himachal)  │  End: Dehradun (Uttarakhand) │
 │  Difficulty: ★★★★★  Very Challenging (max 4,650m)        │
 │  Budget: ₹18,000–30,000/person (guide + camp package)    │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  THE CONCEPT: Rupin Pass is consistently ranked among
  India's top 5 treks. It starts in Sangla Valley
  (Himachal Pradesh) and crosses into Uttarakhand via
  a dramatic 4,650m pass above a frozen waterfall. The
  trek combines Kinnauri valley culture, hanging glaciers,
  snowbridges, and a waterfall so tall it disappears
  into cloud. Very few Indian treks match it.

  WAYPOINT  ①  DAY 1 — SANGLA → SEWA (DEYARA)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Drive from Sangla to the trek head at Sewa village
  (also called Deyara). 22 km from Sangla on a rough
  road — jeep required.

  Trek begins in Baspa Valley farmland. Within 2 km
  you are in pine and oak forest. Camp at Sewa meadow.
  Altitude: 2,680m. Distance: 7 km. Time: 3 hours.

  WAYPOINT  ②  DAY 2 — SEWA → RUPIN VILLAGE → JHAKA
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Trek through the Rupin river valley. The river is
  a shade of blue-green that seems digitally enhanced
  but is entirely real (glacial minerals).

  Rupin village: 20 stone houses. The villagers grow
  potatoes and buckwheat above 3,000m. They have done
  so for generations.

  Jhaka camp at 3,140m. Distance: 11 km. Time: 5 hrs.

  WAYPOINT  ③  DAY 3 — JHAKA → LOWER WATERFALL CAMP
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The day the trek becomes legendary. Trail climbs
  steeply through silver birch to the base of the
  Rupin Waterfall — a multi-tiered cascade dropping
  nearly 300m in a narrow gorge. In May–June the
  lower section is still frozen into a solid ice ramp
  that trekkers walk across. Crampons required early
  season.

  Camp directly below the waterfall. The sound through
  the night is constant, enormous, and somehow calming.

  Altitude: 3,660m. Distance: 8 km. Time: 5–6 hours.

  WAYPOINT  ④  DAY 4 — WATERFALL CAMP → UPPER CAMP
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The climb past the frozen waterfall is the most
  technical section of the trek. There are fixed ropes
  in places. Your guide earns their fee today.

  Above the waterfall: a completely different world —
  wide snowfields, total silence, the pass visible ahead.
  Camp at the upper snowfield at 4,100m.

  Distance: 5 km. Time: 5–7 hours. Pack light for today.

  WAYPOINT  ⑤  DAY 5 — SUMMIT DAY: RUPIN PASS (4,650m)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Leave camp at 5 AM. The pass crossing is 3 km from
  camp but gains 550m. In early season: deep snow,
  crampons mandatory. In September: rocky trail, easier.

  At the summit: you step from Himachal Pradesh into
  Uttarakhand. The view north is Kinnaur and Spiti.
  The view south is the Tons River valley descending
  into the Garhwal Himalaya. Two states, two landscapes,
  one ridge.

  Descent to Ronti Gad camp (Uttarakhand side). 3,900m.
  Distance: 9 km. Time: 7–8 hours total.

  WAYPOINTS  ⑥–⑧  DAYS 6–8 — DESCENT TO DHAULA + OUT
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Three days of steady descent through the Uttarakhand
  side: Ronti Gad → Dhaula village (road head) → bus
  or jeep to Dehradun.

  The Uttarakhand side is greener, more forested, more
  populated — a warm contrast to the Himachali side.

  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  TREK AT A GLANCE
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Day  Campsite          Alt.     Dist.   Time
  1    Sewa Meadow        2,680m   7 km    3 hrs
  2    Jhaka              3,140m  11 km    5 hrs
  3    Lower Waterfall    3,660m   8 km    6 hrs
  4    Upper Snowfield    4,100m   5 km    6 hrs
  5    Ronti Gad          3,900m   9 km    8 hrs ← PASS
  6    Morinda Thatch     3,450m  10 km    5 hrs
  7    Dhaula             2,400m  13 km    6 hrs
  8    Dehradun exit      ——       ——       ——
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  MANDATORY: Registered guide (₹1,800–2,200/day)
  GEAR: Crampons + ice axe (May–June), trekking poles,
        -15°C sleeping bag, full rain gear, gaiters
  AGENCY: Book through Sangla-based operators or
          reputed outfitters in Delhi/Dehradun
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ON THE PLATE: All camp food provided by cook. En route:
  Sangla trout before starting, Kinnauri dried apricots
  as trail snack, Hot soup at every camp, Dark chocolate
  (altitude blunts appetite — eat anyway).

 │  KALATOP & DALHOUSIE — THE CANTONMENT IN THE CLOUDS      │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  Days: 4  │  Region: Dalhousie, Kalatop, Chamba district  │
 │  Theme: Colonial hill station + wildlife forest walks     │
 │  Season: March–June, October–November (Dec–Feb for snow)  │
 │  Start: Pathankot  │  Difficulty: ★★☆☆☆  Easy            │
 │  Budget: ₹8,000–15,000/person                             │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  WAYPOINT  ①  PATHANKOT → DALHOUSIE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Distance : 80 km  │  Drive time: 2.5 hours
  Road      : Mostly good, winding final 20 km

  Dalhousie (2,036m) was founded by Lord Dalhousie
  in 1854 as a summer retreat for British troops.
  Five hills, colonial churches, Edwardian bungalows
  with names like "Oakover" and "Moti Tibba." It has
  aged gracefully and refuses to become a resort town.

  FIRST AFTERNOON WALK: Garam Sadak (warm lane) —
  a 5 km loop around the south face of the hill that
  stays sunny even in winter. Pass old British graves,
  a Victorian post office still in use, and the
  Gandhi Chowk where regimental band concerts
  were once held every evening at sunset.

  STAY: Heritage bungalow or hotel, Dalhousie
        ₹1,500–4,000/night

  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  LOCAL SECRET #2                                    │
  │  St. Francis Church (1894) on the Subhash Chowk    │
  │  mall keeps its original Victorian stained glass   │
  │  and pine-wood pews. It is never locked during     │
  │  daylight. Sit inside for 10 minutes. The silence  │
  │  is deep enough to feel structural.                │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  WAYPOINT  ②  KALATOP WILDLIFE SANCTUARY (FULL DAY)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Distance : 9 km from Dalhousie  │  Entry: ₹30/person

  Kalatop is a high-altitude forest reserve (2,440m)
  covering 31 sq km of dense oak, rhododendron, and
  deodar. Unlike most Himachal forests, it has a
  genuinely functioning wildlife population: Himalayan
  black bear, barking deer, flying squirrel, red fox,
  leopard (rarely seen), and over 150 bird species
  including the Khalij pheasant and various woodpeckers.

  The walking trails are marked and maintained.
  No vehicles allowed inside — walk or horse.
  The forest rest house inside the sanctuary is one
  of the best-located accommodations in all of
  Dalhousie's environs.

  MORNING WALK: Kalatop circular trail (7 km, 3 hours).
  Take the upper ridge path — the Pir Panjal views
  from the ridgeline are spectacular.

  AFTERNOON: Rest house lawn. Read. Watch deer graze
  at the forest edge at dusk. This is the whole plan.

  STAY: Kalatop Forest Rest House  ₹600–1,200
        (book via HP Forest Dept, Dalhousie)
        OR return to Dalhousie hotel

  WAYPOINT  ③  KHAJJIAR (DAY 3)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Distance : 24 km from Dalhousie  │  Drive: 45 min

  Khajjiar has been called "Mini Switzerland" since a
  Swiss diplomat made the comparison in 1992 and
  erected a signboard. The signboard is still there.
  The comparison is fair: a perfectly circular meadow,
  ringed by deodar forest, with a small lake at the
  centre and the Dhauladhar range as backdrop.

  Morning: walk the meadow perimeter (2 km circuit).
  Horse riding, paragliding and zorbing all available
  at the meadow — pick what fits your group.

  Afternoon: visit the Khajji Nag temple on the meadow
  edge — a wooden nagari-style temple with a golden
  dome that appears incongruously ornate in the forest.

  Evening: back to Dalhousie. Sunset from Bakrota
  Hills viewpoint — the entire Chamba valley is visible
  and the Ravi River glints silver far below.

  STAY: Dalhousie  ₹1,500–4,000

  WAYPOINT  ④  CHAMBA TOWN + DEPARTURE (DAY 4)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Distance : 56 km from Dalhousie  │  Drive: 1.5 hours

  Chamba (996m) on the Ravi River: one of Himachal's
  most historically significant towns. The Lakshmi
  Narayan Temple complex — six sikhara temples from
  the 10th–11th century — is the finest example of
  Pahari temple architecture in the state. Walk the
  Chaugan (town meadow) used for the annual Minjar
  Festival (July–August) for a thousand years.

  Bhuri Singh Museum: the best collection of Pahari
  miniature paintings outside of major Delhi museums.
  Paintings here show Kangra and Chamba court life
  from the 17th–19th century in extraordinary detail.

  Return to Pathankot by evening (90 km, 2.5 hours).

  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  DALHOUSIE QUICK REFERENCE
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Nearest railway  : Pathankot (80 km)
  Nearest airport  : Gaggal/Dharamshala (120 km)
  ATMs             : Dalhousie town — 2 working ATMs
  Mobile network   : Good in Dalhousie; patchy Kalatop
  Best time        : May (rhododendron bloom) or Oct
                     (clear skies, Dhauladhar snow)
  Avoid            : Mid-July to mid-August (peak rain)
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ON THE PLATE: Chamba Chukh (fermented red chilli
  paste — buy a jar at Chamba market), Madra, Chamba
  sweets at the Chaugan shops, Dalhousie bakery cakes
  (colonial-era recipe bakeries still operating),
  Rajma from the Chamba valley (different variety
  to Kullu — smaller, darker, more intense flavour).




 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  ROUTE CARD  № 24                                          │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  BARA BHANGAL — THE VALLEY BEYOND ALL ROADS               │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  Days: 9  │  Region: Kangra → Bara Bhangal → Kullu        │
 │  Theme: Remote trans-Dhauladhar crossing                  │
 │  Season: JUNE–SEPTEMBER only                              │
 │  Start: Bir / Billing  │  End: Manali or Kullu            │
 │  Difficulty: ★★★★★  Extreme (one of HP's hardest)        │
 │  Budget: ₹22,000–38,000/person                            │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  THE PREMISE: Bara Bhangal is a high-altitude village
  (2,540m) in the Ravi watershed, accessible only on foot
  across the Dhauladhar range. It has no road. No phone
  signal. No electricity grid. The ~200 Gaddi shepherd
  families who live here winter at lower elevations and
  return each summer. You reach them by crossing a
  4,700m+ pass from the Kangra side. This is not a trek
  for casual mountain walkers. It is an expedition.

  WAYPOINT  ①  DAYS 1–2 — BIR → BILLING → THAMSAR PASS
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Day 1: Drive from Bir to Billing (9 km), the paragliding
  take-off plateau at 2,400m. This is your trek head.

  The Thamsar Pass route climbs directly through the
  Dhauladhar from the Bir side. Day 1 camp is at
  Rajgundha meadow (2,900m) after a 12 km walk through
  oak and rhododendron forest. The Rajgundha meadow
  is one of the most beautiful hidden valleys in
  Kangra — green, wide, surrounded by peaks, used
  by Gaddi shepherds every summer.

  Day 2: Continue from Rajgundha toward Thamsar Pass
  (4,708m). Camp below the pass at 4,200m.

  STAY: Camp, Rajgundha meadow  (guide arranges)

  WAYPOINT  ②  DAY 3 — THAMSAR PASS CROSSING
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  This is the crux. The pass is at 4,708m and the final
  ascent is steep snow/scree. Your guide must know this
  route — it is not well-marked and weather changes fast.

  From the summit: Kangra valley disappears behind you.
  The view north opens into the unknown Ravi watershed.
  The descent toward Bara Bhangal is long and steep —
  allow 8–10 hours for the full crossing day.

  Camp on the northern side at ~3,600m.

  WAYPOINT  ③  DAY 4 — ARRIVE BARA BHANGAL
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Trek down to Bara Bhangal village (2,540m). 10 km,
  4–5 hours through a valley that gradually greens as
  you descend.

  The village is unlike anything on the normal Himachal
  circuit. The Gaddi community here are semi-nomadic
  pastoralists who have lived this pattern for centuries.
  Stone houses. Spinning wool. Children who have never
  seen a city. Elders who speak Gaddi, no Hindi.

  Rest day in Bara Bhangal. Eat what they eat. Sleep
  when it gets dark — no electricity, no blue-screen
  light. Fall asleep to the sound of the Ravi River.

  WAYPOINT  ④  DAYS 5–6 — RAVI RIVER VALLEY WALK
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The trail south from Bara Bhangal follows the Ravi
  River upstream through increasingly dramatic gorge
  country. No other trekkers. No infrastructure.
  Two days of pure wilderness.

  Camp at shepherd shelters along the way — your guide
  knows each one.

  WAYPOINT  ⑤  DAYS 7–9 — KALDANG PASS → KULLU SIDE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The exit route goes over Kaldang Pass (or alternate
  Kalihani Pass, 4,319m) into the Kullu Valley above
  Manali. This is the traditional Gaddi migration route.

  Day 7: Camp below Kaldang Pass.
  Day 8: Cross pass, descend to Kullu valley meadows.
  Day 9: Trek out to the road, taxi to Manali or Kullu.

  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THIS TREK REQUIRES
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ✦ An experienced local guide from Bir or Billing
    who has done this specific route (do not accept
    a guide who hasn't — the navigation is complex)
  ✦ 9 days of food for 2 people minimum
    (buy in Pathankot or Chandigarh)
  ✦ Satellite communicator (SPOT/Garmin inReach) —
    no mobile signal for 7 of 9 days
  ✦ Comprehensive travel insurance with helicopter
    evacuation cover (mandatory, not optional)
  ✦ Physical fitness: 15+ km mountain walks daily,
    two 4,500m+ pass crossings, 9 days pack weight
  ✦ High-clearance camping gear: -15°C bag, 4-season
    tent, crampons for early season
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  LOCAL SECRET #3                                    │
  │  The Gaddi community at Bara Bhangal still          │
  │  practices a form of oral epic storytelling         │
  │  around fires at night — long narrative poems       │
  │  about the mountains, the gods, and the sheep.      │
  │  If you arrive respectfully and your guide          │
  │  speaks Gaddi, you may be invited to listen.        │

  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  ON THE PLATE: Everything you carry. Supplement with
  Gaddi-offered milk, butter, and lassi at Bara Bhangal
  (accept always — refusing is impolite). Dried apricots
  and walnuts as trail food. Bring enough coffee.

 │  ROUTE CARD  № 25                                          │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  UPPER KULLU — CHANDERKHANI PASS & MALANA VILLAGE        │
 │  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   │
 │  Days: 6  │  Region: Naggar → Chanderkhani → Malana       │
 │  Theme: Mountain pass + the most isolated republic        │
 │          in India                                         │
 │  Season: MAY–OCTOBER                                      │
 │  Start: Naggar (Kullu Valley)  │  Difficulty: ★★★★☆      │
 │  Budget: ₹11,000–19,000/person                            │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  THE BACKSTORY: Malana village sits at 3,029m in a
  side valley of the Parvati, accessible only on foot.
  Its people consider themselves descendants of
  Alexander the Great's soldiers and operate as a
  self-governing republic with their own laws, language
  (Kanashi — unintelligible to all outsiders), and
  an ancient two-chamber parliament. They do not
  permit outsiders to touch their walls, temples, or
  people. Their deity Jamlu Devta is the supreme
  authority. The Indian Constitution technically
  applies but is politely ignored.

  You are going there. Respect every rule.

  WAYPOINT  ①  DAY 1 — NAGGAR ACCLIMATISE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Arrive Naggar (1,851m) from Kullu or Manali (20 km).
  This is your base. Rest, eat, walk the Naggar Castle
  grounds and the Roerich Gallery. Sleep early.

  STAY: Naggar guesthouse or castle hotel  ₹1,200–6,000

  WAYPOINT  ②  DAYS 2–3 — NAGGAR → RUMSU → CHANDERKHANI
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Day 2: Drive or walk 6 km to Rumsu village (2,064m)
  — traditional Kullu village with carved wooden houses.
  Trek begins properly here. Trail climbs through
  deodar forest to the ridge above.

  Camp at Narayan Tibba meadow (3,650m). Distance:
  14 km, 6–7 hours. Substantial climb — take it slow.

  Day 3: Rest/acclimatisation day at meadow camp.
  Short exploration walk to the ridgeline. The view
  from Narayan Tibba: the entire Kullu Valley below,
  Deo Tibba (6,001m) ahead, and the Parvati Valley
  opening to the east. 360° panorama.

  STAY: Camp at meadow  (guide arranges)

  WAYPOINT  ③  DAY 4 — CHANDERKHANI PASS CROSSING
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Chanderkhani Pass: 3,660m. The climb from camp is
  3 km of open ridge — windy, exposed, stunning.

  At the pass: the Parvati Valley opens dramatically
  below. Malana village is visible as a cluster of
  grey stone rooftops on a spur 600m below.

  Descent to Malana: 6 km, 2–3 hours, steep rocky trail.

  Arrive Malana afternoon. RULES before entering:
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ① Do not touch any wall, house, temple, or person
  ② Do not enter the inner village without a guide
     who knows the current rules (they change)
  ③ Photography of people only with explicit consent
  ④ All payments in cash — keep notes clean, unfolded
  ⑤ Do not bring beef products into the village
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  STAY: Designated tourist guesthouses OUTSIDE the
  inner village perimeter  ₹600–1,200

  WAYPOINT  ④  DAY 5 — MALANA VILLAGE EXPLORATION
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Morning: walk the outer village lanes with your
  guide. The architecture is extraordinary — massive
  slate-roofed wooden houses stacked on the hillside,
  carved pillars, narrow lanes wide enough for one
  person. The Jamlu Devta temple at the village
  centre is the most sacred spot — observe only,
  do not approach.

  Ask your guide about the parliament system — Malana
  has operated a two-chamber legislative body for
  centuries, with an upper and lower house of elders
  who vote on village matters including whether to
  allow outsiders. Their legal system predates any
  Indian state institution.

  Afternoon: walk to the viewpoint above the village
  for the full panorama of the Malana Nala, the
  Chanderkhani Ridge above, and the Parvati Valley
  gorge below.

  WAYPOINT  ⑤  DAY 6 — MALANA → KASOL (DESCENT)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Trek down the Malana Nala gorge to the Parvati
  Valley road (12 km, 4 hours, very steep in places).
  The trail passes the Malana hydroelectric project
  and emerges at Jari village on the Bhuntar–Kasol road.

  Take a local bus or taxi 10 km to Kasol. Hot shower.
  Real restaurant. Reconnection with the modern world
  after 5 days of mountains and ancient republics.

  Return to Bhuntar Airport (30 km) or Kullu town.

  MALANA ESSENTIAL NOTES
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Language    : Kanashi (unique — not Sanskrit-derived,
                not related to any other Himalayan
                language, origin disputed by linguists)
  Economy     : Sheep herding, some agriculture, and
                the worldwide reputation of Malana
                Cream (no further comment required)
  Legal status: Operates own customary law; Indian
                courts rarely intervene
  Fine system : Touching a temple wall = fine paid
                to Jamlu Devta's account (enforced)
  Hospitality : Warm but boundaried — respect is
                returned with respect, always
