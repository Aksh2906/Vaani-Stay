                    HIMACHAL TRAVEL DIARIES
                   Itineraries 11 through 15
          [ Written as field notes from the road ]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━




╭─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
│  DIARY No. 11                                           │
│  KASAULI & SOLAN — THE QUIET CANTONMENT               │
│                                                         │
│  2 Days  ·  Easy  ·  ₹5,000–9,000/person              │
│  Best Season: October–March  ·  Start: Chandigarh      │
╰─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯

  We left Chandigarh at eight in the morning. By ten-thirty
  we were in Kasauli, and I remember thinking: this is what
  Shimla must have felt like in 1930. Nobody had told us
  about Kasauli because nobody who loves it wants it to
  change. It is a British cantonment from 1842, still
  governed by cantonment rules, which means: no new
  construction, no loud music, no cars on the upper
  mall. The Monkey Point ridge at the northern tip is the
  highest point — an air force installation crowns it,
  but the trail up through dense oak is open to walkers.
  From the top, Chandigarh's grid is visible on the plains
  sixty kilometres away, like a circuit board.

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 1  //  KASAULI

  10:30   Arrive. Drop bags at one of the old colonial
          bungalow guesthouses on the upper mall.
          (Kasauli Club Road has the best ones —
          book ahead, rooms fill fast on weekends.)

  11:00   Walk the Upper Mall. It is lined entirely with
          old deodars and colonial-era bungalows with
          names like "Rose Cottage" and "Fernlea."
          The cantonement church (Christ Church, 1844)
          is always unlocked.

  13:00   Lunch at one of the small restaurants near
          the main market. The momos here are better
          than you expect from such a quiet town.

  15:00   Monkey Point trail — 3 km round trip through
          forest. Permits required from the cantonment
          gate (₹20, carry ID). The Hanuman temple at
          the summit is simple and old.

  17:00   Gilbert Trail — a gentler evening forest walk,
          named after an old British surveyor. Completely
          flat, barely anyone uses it.

  19:30   Sunset drinks at the Kasauli Resort terrace
          or the Kasauli Club (membership required, but
          day passes sometimes available — ask at gate).

  STAY:   Heritage bungalow guesthouse, Kasauli
          ₹2,500–5,500/night

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 2  //  SOLAN + MOHAN SHAKTI HERITAGE PARK + DEPART

  08:00   Drive 20 km to Solan. This town is famous for
          two things: Solan Brewery (Asia's oldest, est.
          1855 — tours available on weekdays) and
          mushroom cultivation (it produces 90% of
          Himachal's mushrooms).

  10:00   Mohan Shakti National Heritage Park, Solan —
          a recently built complex replicating 108 major
          Hindu temple styles from across India in one
          campus. Strange, ambitious, and oddly moving.

  12:30   Lunch in Solan. Try the local brewery beer if
          the tour is timed right.

  14:00   Jatoli Shiv Temple, 7 km from Solan — said to
          be the tallest Shiva temple in Asia (South
          East Asian architecture, not North Indian).
          The craftsmanship is obsessive in its detail.

  16:00   Drive back to Chandigarh (65 km, 2 hours).

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  LOGISTICS AT A GLANCE
  ┌────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Getting there  │ Chandigarh → Kasauli: 65 km, taxi   │
  │                │ ₹1,500–2,000 one way                │
  │ Local travel   │ Walk everywhere in Kasauli upper    │
  │                │ mall — cars not allowed             │
  │ ATM            │ At Kasauli market (one only)        │
  │ Network        │ Good Jio/Airtel coverage            │
  │ Avoid          │ Weekends in peak summer (very       │
  │                │ crowded for such a small place)     │
  └────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘

  ON THE PLATE: Beer from Solan Brewery, Pahari rajma,
  Mushroom dishes (Solan specialty), British-style
  baked goods from the cantonment bakery, Rhododendron
  jam on toast.




╭─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
│  DIARY No. 12                                           │
│  JIBHI & SHOJA — THE SLOW VALLEY                      │
│                                                         │
│  4 Days  ·  Easy–Moderate  ·  ₹8,000–15,000/person   │
│  Best Season: Mar–Jun, Sep–Nov, Dec–Feb (snow)         │
│  Start: Aut Junction (Manali–Chandigarh Highway)       │
╰─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯

  The bus from Chandigarh dropped us at Aut at midnight.
  We slept in a dhabha. By eight the next morning a shared
  taxi had us winding up the Banjar valley road, and by
  ten we understood why everyone who comes here stays
  longer than planned. Jibhi is a village of wooden
  bridges and clear streams where the water runs cold
  and green over white pebbles. The deodar trees are
  massive — not saplings but ancient columns of timber
  that have watched generations of hill people farm
  the same terraced fields. There are no resorts.
  There are homestays run by families who will feed
  you more than you can eat for ₹200 a meal.

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 1  //  ARRIVE JIBHI + EXPLORE

  Reach Jibhi by morning. Drop your bag and walk. That
  is the entire plan for today. Walk to the Jibhi
  Waterfall (1.5 km trail through forest — emerald
  water, mossy rocks). Walk back through the village
  lanes. Sit by the Tirthan tributary river with your
  feet in the water. Drink chai at the shop run by
  the old man whose family has been here for four
  generations. Ask him about the valley in monsoon —
  he will paint you a picture no travel guide can match.

  Evening: bonfire at the homestay if the host allows.
  The night sky in Jibhi is extraordinary — no light
  pollution, no cloud cover in autumn.

  STAY:  Wooden homestay, Jibhi  ₹1,000–2,000 with meals

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 2  //  SHOJA + JALORI PASS + RAGHUPUR FORT

  Shoja sits 10 km uphill from Jibhi — the road climbs
  through forest and arrives at a village balanced on a
  ridge with views in all directions. In December this
  ridge is buried in snow and completely transformed.

  Drive or walk to Jalori Pass (3,120m) — one of
  Himachal's most accessible high passes. The meadow
  at the top is surrounded by silver fir trees that
  stand like sculptures in the mist.

  Trek from Jalori: 45 minutes east to Raghupur Fort —
  a ruined medieval hilltop fortress with panoramic
  views stretching to the Kinnaur range on a clear day.
  Nobody is ever there. You will have the ruins to
  yourself.

  Return to Shoja for the night.
  STAY:  Guesthouse in Shoja  ₹800–1,500

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 3  //  SEROLSAR LAKE TREK

  From Jalori Pass: trek 5 km through dense alpine
  forest to Serolsar Lake (3,199m). The trail is
  narrow, dark, cathedral-like under the silver fir
  canopy. The lake itself is perfectly still, ringed
  by trees, with the tiny Budhi Nagini temple on
  the far shore. According to local belief, birds
  remove any leaf that falls into the water to keep
  it pure. Whether or not you believe it, the lake
  looks impossibly clean.

  Return to Jibhi by afternoon.

  Evening: cook with your homestay host. Learn to make
  Himachali rajma the proper way — slow, in mustard
  oil, with a tempering of dry red chillies and hing.

  STAY:  Back to Jibhi homestay

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 4  //  SHANGARH MEADOW + DEPARTURE

  Drive 20 km from Jibhi to Shangarh — a flat green
  meadow perched impossibly high above the Sainj valley,
  ringed by snow peaks, with a small temple at its
  edge. It is the kind of place that makes you feel
  the earth is slightly tilted in your favour.

  Spend two hours walking the meadow. Have packed
  lunch. Then return to Aut for the bus or taxi
  onward to Chandigarh, Manali, or Kullu.

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  THINGS NOBODY TELLS YOU ABOUT JIBHI
  → The off-season (November, March) is the best time:
    fewer people, cheaper stays, mist on the valley
  → Carry enough cash for 4 days — no ATM in Jibhi
  → Do not plan more than two activities per day here.
    The point is to slow down.
  → The Banjar market (8 km from Jibhi) has the best
    local produce — wild honey, dried apricots, hemp
    fabric items made by local women's cooperatives

  ON THE PLATE: Pahadi rajma, Sidu with ghee, Wild
  honey straight from the jar, Trout from the Tirthan,
  Rhododendron squash, Fresh walnut halwa.




╭─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
│  DIARY No. 13                                           │
│  KULLU VALLEY — THE DUSSEHRA CIRCUIT                  │
│                                                         │
│  7 Days  ·  Easy–Moderate  ·  ₹12,000–20,000/person  │
│  Best Season: October (Dussehra) OR April–May         │
│  Start: Bhuntar Airport / Kullu bus stand              │
╰─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯

  October in Kullu is unlike anywhere else in India.
  The entire valley — every village, every hillside —
  converges on the Dhalpur Maidan in Kullu town for
  the International Dussehra festival, and what happens
  there is not the burning of a Ravana effigy the way
  North India does it. In Kullu, 365 local gods are
  carried in ornate palanquins (raths) from their
  village temples down to the maidan, where they
  gather to pay tribute to the presiding deity Raghunath.
  The procession on the first day is one of the most
  extraordinary things you will witness in India: drums,
  flutes, deity umbrellas, hill people in traditional
  dress, gods meeting gods.

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 1  //  ARRIVE KULLU + RAGHUNATH TEMPLE

  Land at Bhuntar Airport or arrive by bus. Kullu
  town is 10 km north. Check in, then visit the
  Raghunath Temple — the presiding deity of Kullu
  Dussehra. Small, ancient, deeply active with pilgrims.

  Walk the Akhara Bazaar — Kullu's main market, selling
  the famous Kullu shawls woven on handlooms. Watch a
  weaver at work if you find a cooperative open.

  Evening: Riverside walk on the Beas embankment.
  The river is wide and fast here. Watch the sunset
  paint the Hanogi hills across the valley.

  STAY:  Hotel in Kullu  ₹1,200–2,500

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 2  //  BIJLI MAHADEV + BHRIGU TEMPLE

  Bijli Mahadev temple sits at 2,460m on a ridge above
  Kullu — accessible by road (14 km) or trek (5 km).
  The temple has a 20-metre wooden staff that attracts
  lightning. After each strike, the Shivalinga inside
  shatters; the priest reassembles it with butter and
  sattoo. The staff has been struck hundreds of times.

  Afternoon: Bhrigu temple at Raison village (12 km
  south of Kullu) — a riverside temple complex where
  sage Bhrigu is said to have meditated. Peaceful,
  rarely visited.

  STAY:  Kullu  ₹1,200–2,500

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 3  //  NAGGAR VILLAGE (FULL DAY)

  Drive 21 km north to Naggar — the ancient capital of
  the Kullu kingdom before the rajas moved to Kullu
  town in the 1600s. The castle is built using the
  earthquake-resistant kath-kuni technique: alternate
  layers of stone and deodar beams, no mortar.

  Morning: Naggar Castle + Roerich Art Gallery. The
  Russian painter Nicholas Roerich's Himalayan canvases
  cover entire walls. His house and studio are preserved
  exactly as he left them in 1947.

  Afternoon: Walk uphill from Naggar through the village
  to the Murlidhar Temple and then on to the Gauri
  Shankar Temple — ancient carved stone, quiet courtyards
  full of marigolds, locals who barely notice visitors.

  The view from Naggar's upper lanes over the entire
  Kullu Valley is one of the best in Himachal.

  STAY:  Naggar Castle Hotel or local guesthouse
         ₹1,200–6,000

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 4  //  KULLU DUSSEHRA — THE PROCESSION DAY
             [October visitors only — otherwise substitute
              with Parvati Valley day trip]

  The opening procession of Dussehra happens on the day
  after Vijaya Dashami. The Raghunath deity is brought
  out first, then the 364 other gods arrive from their
  villages. The drums begin before dawn and do not
  stop. By the time the procession reaches the Dhalpur
  Maidan at midday, the crowd numbers in the hundreds
  of thousands. Find a spot on an elevated wall or
  building terrace early — the narrow lanes make
  ground-level visibility difficult.

  Evening: the maidan is transformed into a fair with
  folk music, food stalls, and regional craft exhibitions.
  The god-palanquins are arrayed in rows on the field —
  walking among them at dusk, with oil lamps lit, is
  genuinely surreal.

  STAY:  Kullu (book 3 months ahead for Dussehra week)

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 5  //  KHOKHAN + RUMSU VILLAGE

  Khokhan (5 km from Bhuntar): a forgotten village
  surrounded by deodar forest and apple orchards.
  The Khokhan Devi temple complex here is one of the
  finest examples of Himalayan wooden temple carving
  in the valley. Almost nobody visits.

  Rumsu village (2,064m, 14 km from Kullu): wooden
  houses built like fairy tale sets, stone lanes,
  a village economy unchanged since the 1960s.
  The people are warm and extremely surprised to
  see visitors.

  STAY:  Kullu or farmstay near Bhuntar  ₹1,000–2,500

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 6  //  TATTAPANI HOT SPRINGS + RAFTING

  Drive 45 km south of Kullu to Tattapani on the Sutlej
  River. The natural hot sulphur springs here emerge
  directly on the riverbank — you bathe in hot water
  while the cold Sutlej flows ten metres away.

  The Sutlej at Tattapani offers Grade II–III white
  water rafting (best September–November). Several
  operators on site, 2-hour rafts available.

  STAY:  Kullu or Tattapani camp  ₹800–2,000

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 7  //  SHAWL SHOPPING + DEPARTURE

  Final morning: back to Kullu's Akhara Bazaar and
  the Bhuttico Weavers' Cooperative showroom —
  the most reliable place for authentic Kullu shawls
  at fair prices. Watch the shuttle loom in action.

  Bhuntar Airport flights leave for Delhi at midday.
  Or take the Volvo to Chandigarh (5 hrs).

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  ON THE PLATE: Kullu trout, Siddu, Dham (traditional
  feast plate of rice, dal, rajma, kaddu, and meetha),
  Sepu vadi, local apple cider (informal — ask locals),
  Aktori (buckwheat pancake with honey).




╭─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
│  DIARY No. 14                                           │
│  WINTER MANALI — SNOW, SOLITUDE & HOT SPRINGS         │
│                                                         │
│  5 Days  ·  Easy  ·  ₹14,000–24,000/person           │
│  Best Season: December–February (peak snow)            │
│  Start: Delhi (overnight Volvo) or Bhuntar Airport     │
╰─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯

  Most people visit Manali in May and complain it is
  crowded. The ones who come in January keep it to
  themselves. In winter Manali becomes two places:
  Manali town (functioning, warm hotels, open restaurants)
  and everything above it — buried, white, silent, and
  startlingly beautiful. Rohtang is closed. Solang is
  a snow playground. Old Manali is a ghost version of
  itself, its summer restaurant terraces buried under
  a metre of snow. This itinerary leans into all of it.

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 1  //  ARRIVE + VASHISHT HOT SPRINGS

  Arrive Manali morning. Check in — winter hotel rates
  are 40–60% below summer prices and rooms are yours
  to negotiate. Immediately go to Vashisht village
  (3 km from Mall Road).

  The Vashisht hot springs are ancient — two stone
  bathing tanks fed by thermal vents that maintain
  a constant 40°C. In January, sitting in that water
  while snow falls around you is an experience that
  is difficult to describe to anyone who hasn't done it.

  Afternoon: Walk Old Manali. The summer crowds are gone.
  The shawl vendors are still there, wrapped in their
  own goods, willing to negotiate. The bakeries are open
  and warmer than anywhere else in town.

  STAY:  Hotel near Mall Road or Old Manali  ₹1,500–4,000
         (heated rooms are worth the extra ₹500)

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 2  //  SOLANG VALLEY SNOW DAY

  Drive 13 km north to Solang Valley — the snow bowl
  above Manali that fills with families and adventure
  tourists every December–February.

  Activities available (season-dependent):
  → Skiing: beginner slopes maintained by HPTDC,
    equipment rental on site (₹500–800/day)
  → Snow tube / zorbing on snow (₹200–400)
  → Snowmobile rides across the valley (₹600–1,000)
  → Just walking in deep snow in total quiet —
    which costs nothing and is better than all of
    the above

  The Atal Tunnel (8.9 km, opened 2020) connects
  Solang to Sissu in Lahaul — if you want a ghost-
  valley experience, drive through it to Sissu, now
  completely snowbound and abandoned for winter,
  and back.

  STAY:  Manali  ₹1,500–4,000

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 3  //  HADIMBA TEMPLE + SNOW TREK TO JOGNI FALLS

  Morning: Hadimba Devi Temple in the deodar grove —
  the four-tiered pagoda roof is half buried in snow
  drifts by January. The grove is silent. The carved
  wooden doorway is 500 years old.

  Afternoon: Trek to Jogni Waterfall (4 km return from
  Vashisht). The trail follows a stream uphill through
  snow-dusted forest. In winter the falls are partially
  frozen — icicle columns along the rock face, water
  still pushing through. Few people make this walk
  in January. You will likely be alone.

  Evening: Try the famous winter dining scene at one
  of Old Manali's remaining open restaurants — Johnson
  Bar or Drifter's Inn. Hot wine. Fireplace. Deserve it.

  STAY:  Manali  ₹1,500–4,000

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 4  //  NAGGAR CASTLE IN SNOW + KULLU VALLEY DRIVE

  Drive down the Kullu Valley to Naggar (37 km).
  In winter the valley is at its most cinematic —
  bare apple trees, frost on the terraced fields,
  the peaks absolutely sharp in the cold dry air.

  Naggar Castle in snow: the old deodar beams grey
  against white, the courtyard empty, the Roerich
  Gallery peaceful in a way it is not in summer.

  Lunch on the castle terrace with a direct view
  down the entire Kullu Valley — one of the great
  lunches in the Himalayas.

  Return to Manali for the last night.

  STAY:  Manali  ₹1,500–4,000

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 5  //  TIBETAN MONASTERY CIRCUIT + DEPARTURE

  Morning: Himalayan Nyingmapa Gompa (Manali's main
  Buddhist monastery), the Gadhan Thekchhokling
  Gompa, and the small Tibetan market behind it —
  winter gives the market an intimate quality that
  summer crowds destroy.

  Afternoon: Board the overnight Volvo to Delhi
  (departs ~4–5 PM, arrives Delhi 7 AM). Or drive
  to Bhuntar Airport (30 km).

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  WINTER MANALI SURVIVAL NOTES
  → Minimum temperature in January: -8 to -15°C at night
  → Carry: base layers + mid-layer fleece + down jacket
    + waterproof outer + thermal socks (non-negotiable)
  → Rohtang Pass is CLOSED December–May
  → Atal Tunnel is open year-round — Sissu day trip works
  → Most Solang snow activities shut if snowfall is heavy
    the same day — build buffer days if adventure is key
  → Water pipes can freeze — hot water may be limited
    to specific hours (ask your hotel)

  ON THE PLATE: Hot thukpa at any Tibetan café, Maggi
  with extra butter at 2 AM when you can't sleep from
  the cold, Manali apple brandy (local, unofficial —
  ask your hotel owner), Trout fish fry, Seabuckthorn
  juice (local superfruit, bright orange, tastes like
  a citrus punch).




╭─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
│  DIARY No. 15                                           │
│  THE GREAT HIMALAYAN NATIONAL PARK — DEEP FOREST     │
│                                                         │
│  9 Days  ·  Challenging  ·  ₹25,000–40,000/person    │
│  Best Season: May–June, September–October             │
│  Start: Bhuntar Airport / Aut Junction                 │
╰─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯

  The Great Himalayan National Park covers 754 sq km
  of completely protected wilderness — no roads inside,
  no permanent human habitation, no accommodation.
  What exists is a trail system used by a handful of
  trekkers each year and a biodiversity so dense that
  UNESCO added it to the World Heritage List in 2014.
  Snow leopard, brown bear, blue sheep, western tragopan
  (one of the world's rarest pheasants), musk deer —
  the park holds all of them and keeps them hidden
  behind valleys that take real effort to reach.

  This is not a casual trek. It requires a registered
  guide, a camping permit from the GHNP office in
  Shamshi, and a fitness level that can handle
  8–12 km of mountain terrain daily with pack weight.

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAY 1  //  BHUNTAR → GUSHAINI → PERMITS + GEAR CHECK

  Arrive Bhuntar. Taxi to Gushaini (40 km, 1.5 hrs)
  in Tirthan Valley. This quiet riverside village is
  the base for GHNP treks.

  Afternoon: Register at the GHNP entry gate at Sai
  Ropa (6 km from Gushaini). Collect permits (₹200/day
  for foreigners, ₹50/day for Indians). The park rangers
  here are exceptionally knowledgeable — spend an hour
  talking to them about what to expect inside.

  Evening: Check gear with your guide. Pack only what
  you need — every extra kilo compounds over 9 days.

  STAY:  Homestay in Gushaini  ₹1,200–2,000 with meals

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAYS 2–3  //  TIRTHAN VALLEY BUFFER ZONE → ROLLA

  These two days are inside the GHNP buffer zone —
  dense mixed forest of oak, horse chestnut, and
  Himalayan maple. The Tirthan river runs cold and
  clear alongside. Brown trout dart from your shadow.

  The trail to Rolla (8 km from Sai Ropa) passes through
  the buffer zone's most accessible section. A waterfall
  drops 40 metres into a forest pool. The undergrowth
  is subtropical-dense at lower elevations and transitions
  to alpine scrub as you gain altitude.

  Camp at Rolla (2,400m) — one of the few designated
  camp sites. Rangers do periodic checks.

  STAY:  Camp at Rolla  (bring your own tent or rent
         in Gushaini)

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAYS 4–5  //  ROLLA → PUNDRIK LAKE → TIRATH

  Beyond Rolla the trail enters the core GHNP zone.
  The forest becomes old growth — trees that have
  never been logged, fallen trunks rotting into
  carpets of moss, silence broken only by rivers
  and birds.

  Pundrik Lake (3,150m): a sacred lake in the forest
  that almost no trekker visits. The western tragopan
  — the pheasant so rare it is on the GHNP emblem —
  has been sighted near this lake in the mornings.
  Move quietly.

  Continue to Tirath — a high-altitude meadow
  campsite with snow peak views above the treeline.

  STAY:  Camp at Tirath  (4,500m) — warmest sleeping
         bag you own

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAYS 6–7  //  TIRATH → SHILT HUTS → RAKTISAR

  This is the high section. Exposed alpine terrain
  above 3,800m. Blue sheep (bharal) are commonly seen
  on these slopes — they stand on vertical rock faces
  with casual confidence. Snow patches year-round.

  Shilt Huts: old shepherd shelters at 3,900m used
  as emergency overnight stops. The guide knows them.

  Raktisar meadow (4,000m) is the highest campsite
  on this route — an open bowl surrounded by peaks,
  utterly remote, accessible to perhaps a few dozen
  people each year.

  STAY:  Camp at Raktisar or Shilt Huts

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  DAYS 8–9  //  DESCENT → GUSHAINI → DEPARTURE

  Two days of descent, retracing the trail. The
  return always feels different — you see things you
  missed going up. By day 9 you reach Gushaini.

  Final night: hot meal at the homestay. The host
  family will have kept your remaining luggage.
  Sleep in a bed. Feel how much your body has done.

  Next morning: taxi to Bhuntar. Flight or bus home.

  - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
