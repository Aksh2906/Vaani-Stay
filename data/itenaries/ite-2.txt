ITINERARY 1: SPITI — THE COLD DESERT ODYSSEY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Duration:     8 Days
Theme:        High-altitude desert & Buddhist culture
Best Season:  June – September
Difficulty:   Challenging
Starting Point: Shimla
Budget:       ₹18,000 – ₹32,000 per person

OVERVIEW:
Spiti Valley sits at an average altitude of 12,500 ft, making it
one of the most remote and dramatic landscapes in India. This
circuit takes you through ancient monasteries, lunar terrain,
fossilised sea creatures in the rocks, and nights under the
most star-dense skies you will ever see.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DAY-BY-DAY PLAN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Day 1: Shimla → Narkanda → Rampur
  Morning:   Depart Shimla by 7 AM on NH5. Stop at Narkanda
             (2,700m) for breakfast — the apple orchards here
             are glorious in summer.
  Afternoon: Continue to Rampur Bushahr, a historic trade town
             on the Sutlej. Visit the Padam Palace and the
             Lavi Fair grounds.
  Evening:   Stroll along the riverside market. Try local
             street food — Chha Gosht (marinated lamb curry).
  Stay:      Guesthouse in Rampur (₹800–1,200/night)

Day 2: Rampur → Sangla Valley → Chitkul
  Morning:   Drive through Kinnaur into the Baspa Valley.
             The road hugs sheer cliffs above the roaring
             Sutlej — keep your camera ready.
  Afternoon: Reach Sangla (2,680m). Walk through the Bering
             Nag Temple and the old wooden fort of Kamru.
  Evening:   Push ahead to Chitkul (3,450m) — the last
             inhabited village on the Indo-Tibet border.
             Watch the sunset over snow peaks from the
             ancient temple courtyard.
  Stay:      Homestay in Chitkul (₹1,000–1,500/night)

Day 3: Chitkul → Nako → Tabo
  Morning:   Early start. Cross the high moorlands above
             Chitkul before descending to Pooh and the
             famous Karcham–Wangtoo dam area.
  Afternoon: Detour to Nako village (3,662m) and its stunning
             glacial lake — one of the most photogenic spots
             in Kinnaur. The monastery here dates to the
             10th century.
  Evening:   Arrive Tabo (3,050m). Walk around the Tabo
             Monastery complex — a UNESCO tentative site
             with 1,000-year-old murals.
  Stay:      Monastery guesthouse or homestay in Tabo
             (₹800–1,200/night)

Day 4: Tabo → Dhankar → Pin Valley → Kaza
  Morning:   Trek or drive up to Dhankar Monastery (3,890m),
             perched like an eagle's nest on a ridge above
             the confluence of Spiti and Pin rivers.
  Afternoon: Enter Pin Valley National Park — home to the
             Snow Leopard and Siberian ibex. The valley floor
             is carpeted with wildflowers in July–August.
  Evening:   Reach Kaza (3,800m), the district headquarters.
             Explore the market and pick up Spitian thangkas
             or locally spun yak wool.
  Stay:      Hotel/guesthouse in Kaza (₹1,200–2,000/night)

Day 5: Kaza — Key, Kibber, Chicham
  Morning:   Visit Key Monastery (4,166m), the most iconic
             gompa in Spiti — white-washed tiers stacked on
             a rocky hill, monks chanting at dawn.
  Afternoon: Drive to Kibber (4,270m), once listed as the
             world's highest motorable village. Cross the
             Chicham Bridge — Asia's highest suspension
             bridge connecting two cliff faces.
  Evening:   Return to Kaza. Attend evening prayers at a
             local monastery.
  Stay:      Kaza (same as previous night)

Day 6: Kaza → Langza → Hikkim → Komic → Chandratal
  Morning:   Fossil Village Langza: giant ammonite fossils
             from the ancient Tethys Sea lie scattered in
             fields. Hikkim has the world's highest post
             office — send a postcard home.
  Afternoon: Komic, the world's highest motorable village
             (4,587m). Then descend and take the Kunzum
             Pass (4,590m) road toward Chandratal Lake.
  Evening:   Camp at Chandratal (4,300m) — "Moon Lake." The
             turquoise water at sunset turns violet and then
             ink-black. Stars so thick they blur together.
  Stay:      Tent camp near Chandratal (₹1,500–2,500/night)

Day 7: Chandratal → Rohtang Pass → Manali
  Morning:   Wake before sunrise for golden light on the
             lake. Then drive over Kunzum Pass and through
             the Lahaul Valley — wide, wind-scoured and
             beautiful.
  Afternoon: Cross Rohtang Pass (3,978m) into the green
             Kullu Valley — the contrast from the arid Spiti
             side is staggering.
  Evening:   Arrive Manali. Hot shower, real restaurant food,
             a cold Manali beer.
  Stay:      Hotel in Old Manali (₹1,500–3,000/night)

Day 8: Manali — Rest, Vashisht, Departure
  Morning:   Soak in the Vashisht hot sulphur springs.
             Browse the Tibetan market on Mall Road.
  Afternoon: Visit Hadimba Devi Temple in the deodar forest.
             Lunch at a rooftop café in Old Manali.
  Evening:   Overnight Volvo bus to Delhi or fly from
             Bhuntar Airport (30 km away).

TRAVEL TIPS:
- Acclimatise for a night in Narkanda or Rampur before
  going above 3,500m — Spiti is brutal on unprepared lungs.
- Carry cash; ATMs exist only in Kaza and are unreliable.
- Permits: Restricted Area Permit for Chitkul area for
  foreign nationals — obtain in Shimla or Reckong Peo.
- BSNL SIM gives the most coverage; Jio works in Kaza only.
- Pack for -5°C nights even in July.

MUST-TRY FOOD:
Thukpa (noodle soup), Tsampa (roasted barley porridge),
Yak butter tea, Chhang (local barley beer), Momos with
fiery Spitian chutney.

ITINERARY 2: DHARAMSHALA & CHAMBA — DHARMA AND WILDERNESS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Duration:     5 Days
Theme:        Tibetan culture + offbeat Chamba valley
Best Season:  March – June, September – November
Difficulty:   Easy to Moderate
Starting Point: Pathankot / Gaggal Airport
Budget:       ₹10,000 – ₹18,000 per person

OVERVIEW:
Most tourists come to McLeod Ganj and leave. This itinerary
goes deeper — into Dharamsala's Tibetan exile culture, up
to the snow-line at Triund, and then west into the little-
visited Chamba district with its ancient stone temples and
the jaw-dropping meadow of Khajjiar.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DAY-BY-DAY PLAN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Day 1: Arrive Dharamshala → McLeod Ganj
  Morning:   Arrive Gaggal Airport or Pathankot station.
             Check in at McLeod Ganj. Walk the Tsuglagkhang
             Circuit — Dalai Lama's temple, Namgyal Monastery,
             and the Tibet Museum (deeply moving exhibits on
             the 1959 exodus).
  Afternoon: Explore McLeod Ganj market. Buy hand-painted
             Thangkas, singing bowls, and local Tibetan bread.
  Evening:   Dinner at a Tibetan restaurant on Jogibara Road.
             Try Shapta (stir-fried meat) and butter tea.
  Stay:      Guesthouse in McLeod Ganj (₹800–2,000/night)

Day 2: Triund Trek
  Morning:   Start the Triund trek (9 km, 900m ascent) from
             Gallu Devi Temple at 7 AM. The trail winds
             through oak and rhododendron forest.
  Afternoon: Reach Triund meadow (2,828m) — a flat grassy
             ridge with a stunning 180° view of the Dhauladhar
             range right in front of you, like a wall of ice.
  Evening:   Camp overnight at Triund. Watch the light die
             on the snow faces. Campfire if permitted.
  Stay:      Tent camp at Triund (₹500–1,000 for camping)
             or return to McLeod Ganj by sunset.

Day 3: Dharamshala Lower Town → Palampur Tea Gardens
  Morning:   Visit Dharamshala lower town: St. John's Church
             in the Wilderness (1852), and the grave of
             Lord Elgin. Walk through the HPCA Cricket
             Stadium — one of the most scenic grounds on
             earth, Dhauladhar as backdrop.
  Afternoon: Drive 35 km to Palampur. Stroll through the
             Neugal Khad tea estates — rows of pruned bushes
             descending into misty valleys. Visit a tea
             factory and taste fresh Kangra tea.
  Evening:   Back to McLeod Ganj or stay in Palampur.
  Stay:      Tea-garden resort in Palampur (₹2,500–5,000)

Day 4: Drive to Dalhousie → Khajjiar
  Morning:   Drive 2.5 hours to Dalhousie. This is old-school
             hill station Himachal — Edwardian bungalows,
             quiet mall roads, colonial churches. Walk the
             Garam Sadak (warm lane) circuit.
  Afternoon: Continue 24 km to Khajjiar — a saucer-shaped
             meadow ringed by deodar forest with a small
             lake at its centre. Called "Mini Switzerland."
             Horse riding and paragliding available.
  Evening:   Sunset at Khajjiar. The meadow turns gold.
  Stay:      Hotel in Dalhousie or Khajjiar (₹1,500–3,000)

Day 5: Chamba Town → Departure
  Morning:   Drive 56 km to Chamba town on the Ravi River.
             Visit the Lakshmi Narayan Temple Complex — six
             shikhara-style temples dating from 10th–11th
             century. Stunning Pahari stonework.
  Afternoon: Explore the Bhuri Singh Museum — finest
             collection of Pahari miniature paintings in the
             state. Walk the Chaugan (town meadow).
  Evening:   Drive to Pathankot (90 km) for train/bus back.

TRAVEL TIPS:
- The Triund trail is closed during heavy snow (Dec–Feb) —
  check with locals before starting.
- Carry your own sleeping bag for Triund camping.
- Chamba is 2–3 hours from Dalhousie on mountain roads —
  hire a local taxi, not Ola/Uber.
- Register your vehicle at the Rohtang eco-permit gate if
  continuing north.

MUST-TRY FOOD:
Madra (chickpea gravy), Chamba Chukh (fermented red
chilli paste), Dham (full plate festive meal), Kangra
tea with local honey, Sidu (stuffed wheat bread).

ITINERARY 3: PARVATI VALLEY — THE HIPPIE TRAIL DEEP DIVE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Duration:     6 Days
Theme:        Backpacker culture, treks, hot springs,
              cannabis heritage and mountain solitude
Best Season:  May – June, September – October
Difficulty:   Moderate (with one challenging day)
Starting Point: Bhuntar (Kullu-Manali Airport)
Budget:       ₹8,000 – ₹14,000 per person

OVERVIEW:
Parvati Valley is where budget travellers, trekkers,
Israeli backpackers, and Shiva devotees all converge in
one of India's most intense and beautiful valleys. The
river is turquoise, the forests dark and dripping, the
people unhurried. This itinerary balances the social
village life of Kasol with the genuine wilderness of
Kheerganga and beyond.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DAY-BY-DAY PLAN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Day 1: Bhuntar → Manikaran → Kasol
  Morning:   From Bhuntar, take a local bus up the Parvati
             Valley road (1.5 hours, ₹50). Stop at Manikaran
             Sahib — a Sikh Gurudwara built over naturally
             boiling hot springs. The Langar (free community
             meal) here is cooked in the geothermal vents.
  Afternoon: Continue 4 km to Kasol. Drop bags and walk
             the riverside — Israeli restaurants, chillum
             shops, dreadlocked long-termers, and the
             cleanest mountain river you've seen.
  Evening:   Dinner at one of Kasol's famous cafés.
             Moon Dance or Jim Morrison serve excellent
             hummus, shakshuka, and baked goods.
  Stay:      Riverside guesthouse in Kasol (₹500–1,200)

Day 2: Kasol → Chalal → Grahan Village
  Morning:   Short hike (1 hour) to Chalal — a tiny village
             across the river accessible only by footbridge.
             More laid-back than Kasol, popular with week-
             long campers.
  Afternoon: Trek onward 6 km (3–4 hours) to Grahan Village
             (2,400m). This traditional Himachali hamlet is
             rarely visited — stone houses, terraced fields,
             and an ancient temple.
  Evening:   Stay with a local family. Sit by the fire,
             eat dal-chawal, listen to folk stories.
  Stay:      Homestay in Grahan (₹600–900 incl. meals)

Day 3: Grahan → Kheerganga (via forest trail)
  Morning:   Trek through thick fir and birch forest above
             Grahan toward Kheerganga (12 km, 5–6 hours,
             stiff ascent). Less crowded route than the
             standard Barshaini path.
  Afternoon: Reach Kheerganga meadow (3,050m). Natural hot
             spring pools at the top — your aching legs will
             thank you. Soak for an hour.
  Evening:   This is a sunset not easily forgotten — the
             valley below turns violet, the snow peaks
             catch last light. Simple camp meals.
  Stay:      Tent camp at Kheerganga (₹300–600)

Day 4: Kheerganga → Tosh Village
  Morning:   Descend to Barshaini (2 hours) and cross the
             river to begin the ascent to Tosh village
             (2,400m) — 1.5 hour uphill walk.
  Afternoon: Tosh is perched high above the valley on a
             spur — dramatic views in every direction.
             It's quieter than Kasol, artistic, and has
             some of the best guesthouses in the valley.
  Evening:   Sunset from the Pink Floyd Café (legendary
             wooden balcony jutting over the valley).
  Stay:      Guesthouse in Tosh (₹600–1,500)

Day 5: Tosh → Pulga → Kalga
  Morning:   Trek down to Barshaini and up the other side
             to Pulga — a village so small it has maybe
             20 buildings and one shop. Pure, quiet.
  Afternoon: Continue 30 minutes to Kalga, the final
             accessible village in the upper valley. Apple
             trees, flower meadows, and snow peaks ahead.
             Read a book. Do nothing. This is the point.
  Evening:   Walk back to Barshaini for the last bus to
             Kasol or stay the night in Kalga.
  Stay:      Guesthouse in Kalga or return to Kasol

Day 6: Kasol → Bhuntar → Departure
  Morning:   Final riverside walk in Kasol. Buy local
             trout from the fish stalls.
  Afternoon: Bus to Bhuntar for onward journey to Manali
             (90 km north) or Delhi bus from Kullu.

TRAVEL TIPS:
- Don't carry valuables to Kheerganga — the trail involves
  river crossings and steep descents.
- The hot springs at Kheerganga have two pools — one near
  the top is cleaner and less crowded.
- Leeches on the trail post-monsoon — wear gaiters or
  tuck trousers into socks.
- Carry enough cash for 3–4 days; no ATMs above Kasol.
- Respect the local Himachali villages — ask before
  photographing people.

MUST-TRY FOOD:
Rainbow trout (grilled fresh from river), Himachali
rajma, Israeli-style shakshuka, Maggi at 3,000m,
Rhododendron juice, Fresh walnut from the orchards.

ITINERARY 4: KINNAUR — APPLES, CLIFFS AND ANCIENT TEMPLES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Duration:     4 Days
Theme:        Scenic road trip + apple country + temples
Best Season:  April – June, September – November
Difficulty:   Easy (road trip based)
Starting Point: Shimla
Budget:       ₹12,000 – ₹22,000 per person

OVERVIEW:
Kinnaur is Himachal's most dramatic drive — the National
Highway literally clings to vertical cliff faces above the
Sutlej River for 200 km. The landscape shifts from pine
forest to desert canyon to apple orchard within hours.
Kinnauris wear distinctive green caps with a flower and
are among the most fiercely independent mountain people
in India.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DAY-BY-DAY PLAN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Day 1: Shimla → Sarahan (170 km)
  Morning:   Leave Shimla early (6 AM). Drive through Theog
             and Narkanda — beautiful apple belt. Stop at
             Narkanda for breakfast and a short walk to
             Hatu Peak viewpoint (2.5 km trail, panoramic
             Himalayan views including Srikhand Mahadev).
  Afternoon: Continue to Jeori and turn off for Sarahan
             (2,165m). This small town holds the Bhimakali
             Temple — a remarkable fusion of Himalayan and
             Pahari architecture, the deity's silver face
             gleaming in butter lamp light.
  Evening:   Walk the Sarahan apple orchards. In September–
             October they are heavy with fruit and the
             fragrance is overwhelming.
  Stay:      HPTDC Hotel Shrikhand, Sarahan (₹2,500–4,000)

Day 2: Sarahan → Sangla → Chitkul
  Morning:   Early morning Bhimakali Temple darshan. Drive
             on NH5 through the spectacular Sutlej gorge —
             sheer granite walls 1,000m high, river below
             like green rope.
  Afternoon: Branch into Baspa Valley to Sangla (2,680m).
             Visit Kamru Fort and Bering Nag Temple — the
             deity here wears a crown of mountain ibex horns.
  Evening:   Drive 28 km further to Chitkul (3,450m) —
             the last Indian village on the Hindustan-Tibet
             road. The ancient wooden Mathi Devi temple,
             the army post, the stillness.
  Stay:      Homestay in Chitkul (₹1,000–1,800 with meals)

Day 3: Chitkul → Reckong Peo → Kalpa (Sunrise View)
  Morning:   Walk to the mouth of the valley at dawn for
             the view toward Tibet. Return to Sangla and
             drive back to the main highway. Continue to
             Reckong Peo — Kinnaur's administrative hub.
  Afternoon: Drive 11 km up to Kalpa village (2,960m).
             The view of the Kinner Kailash range from
             here — a 6,050m massif with a reported natural
             shivalinga at its summit — is one of the great
             mountain panoramas in all of India.
  Evening:   Stay at Kalpa and watch the moon rise over
             Kinner Kailash. Locals believe the Gods hold
             court on the summit at night.
  Stay:      Kinner Villa or Apple Blossom, Kalpa
             (₹2,000–4,000)

Day 4: Kalpa → Morang → Hangrang Valley → Departure
  Morning:   If time allows, drive up the Hangrang Valley
             road above Peo toward Nako — a hidden village
             with a Tibetan monastery reflected in a high
             altitude lake.
  Afternoon: Return to Reckong Peo and begin the drive
             back to Shimla (170 km, 6–7 hours on mountain
             roads). Arrive Shimla by night.

TRAVEL TIPS:
- The cliff road between Wangtu and Spillow has frequent
  rockfall — do not stop the vehicle in narrow tunnels.
- Obtain Inner Line Permit at Peo for areas beyond Peo
  toward Spiti — Indian nationals exempt but carry ID.
- Petrol stations: fill up at Rampur; the next reliable
  pump is Reckong Peo.
- September–October apple season means the roads can be
  clogged with trucks. Add 1 hour to all drive estimates.
- Night driving on these roads is not recommended.

MUST-TRY FOOD:
Kinnauri Pahari rajma (small, intensely flavoured local
beans), fresh apple (straight from the tree), Chulli
oil (wild apricot kernel oil), Sooji Halwa at the
temple langar, Anardana chutney.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ITINERARY 5: BIR BILLING TO TIRTHAN VALLEY — SKY, RIVER
             AND SILENCE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Duration:     7 Days
Theme:        Paragliding + trout fishing + forest trekking
Best Season:  October – November, March – May
Difficulty:   Easy to Moderate
Starting Point: Chandigarh / Gaggal Airport
Budget:       ₹14,000 – ₹24,000 per person

OVERVIEW:
This circuit links two of Himachal's most underrated
destinations. Bir Billing is the paragliding capital of
Asia — the Billing take-off site at 2,400m and Bir landing
zone in the valley is the longest paragliding route on the
continent. Tirthan Valley, four hours south, is a pristine
river sanctuary inside the Great Himalayan National Park
buffer zone, where brown trout thrive in water so clear
you can count the pebbles ten feet down.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DAY-BY-DAY PLAN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Day 1: Chandigarh → Bir (180 km, 5 hrs)
  Morning:   Drive from Chandigarh via Bilaspur and Mandi.
             The road hugs the Beas river from Mandi — lush
             and gorgeous.
  Afternoon: Arrive Bir (1,525m). Check in, walk the Tibetan
             Colony — one of the largest Tibetan settlements
             in India outside Dharamshala. Beautiful red-
             robed monks, spinning prayer wheels, the smell
             of juniper incense.
  Evening:   Watch paragliders land on the Bir landing ground
             just outside the colony. This is the scene.
  Stay:      Homestay or eco-camp in Bir (₹1,000–2,500)

Day 2: Billing Paragliding Day
  Morning:   Drive up the forest road to Billing take-off
             (2,400m). Tandem paragliding flight with a
             APPI-certified pilot — 20–40 minute flight
             depending on thermals. The view: the entire
             Kangra Valley, Dhauladhar range, and on clear
             days, Manali peaks.
  Afternoon: Optional second flight or hiking around the
             Billing meadow. The grassland plateau is
             spectacular for a picnic.
  Evening:   Back in Bir. Visit the Sherabling Monastery —
             one of the most important Nyingma monasteries
             in India, with stunning frescoes.
  Stay:      Bir (same as previous)

Day 3: Bir → Barot Valley (Day Trip)
  Morning:   Drive 40 km into the Uhl River valley to Barot
             (1,600m) — a remote village around a narrow
             reservoir. Rent a fishing rod from locals and
             try your hand at trout fishing.
  Afternoon: Walk the forest trail along the reservoir to
             the old hydroelectric project. Picnic lunch
             by the water.
  Evening:   Return to Bir. Dinner with a family in the
             Tibetan colony — many offer meals to visitors.
  Stay:      Bir

Day 4: Bir → Jogindernagar → Tirthan Valley (via Aut)
  Morning:   Drive south from Bir, passing through Palampur
             and dropping into the Kullu Valley at Aut.
             Take the road into Sainj valley — this is the
             start of the Great Himalayan National Park
             buffer zone.
  Afternoon: Arrive Gushaini or Banjar in Tirthan Valley
             (1,600m). Walk to the river immediately — the
             Tirthan is everything the photos promised.
             Cold, clear, fast-moving, surrounded by forest.
  Evening:   Sit on the rocks above the river. Have chai
             at a local dhaba. This is one of the most
             peaceful places in India.
  Stay:      River-side homestay in Gushaini (₹1,200–2,500)

Day 5: Tirthan Valley — GHNP Trek
  Morning:   Enter the Great Himalayan National Park on
             a guided day trek (mandatory guide from the
             buffer zone). Trail follows the Tirthan River
             into old-growth forest — oak, horse chestnut,
             Himalayan maple. Chance of seeing serow,
             barking deer, and Himalayan brown bear signs.
  Afternoon: Trek to Rolla waterfall (8 km round trip) or
             continue to Shakti camp if fit. The forest
             here is UNESCO World Heritage Site quality.
  Evening:   Return to homestay. Fresh trout cooked by
             the host family.
  Stay:      Gushaini (same)

Day 6: Jalori Pass and Serolsar Lake
  Morning:   Drive up to Jalori Pass (3,120m) — accessible
             by car unlike most Himalayan passes. Small
             temple at the top with 360° views.
  Afternoon: Trek 5 km (return) to Serolsar Lake — a sacred
             lake in dense forest. Absolutely silent. The
             Budhi Nagin temple at the shore is ancient and
             atmospheric. Golden trout flash in the shallows.
  Evening:   Descend to Banjar. Final evening by the river.
  Stay:      Gushaini / Banjar

Day 7: Tirthan Valley → Chandigarh / Delhi Bus
  Morning:   Final riverside walk. Buy local honey (Tirthan
             is famous for wild mountain honey) and local
             woollen shawls.
  Afternoon: Drive to Aut and join the Manali-Chandigarh
             highway. Chandigarh in 4–5 hours. Delhi
             Volvo bus from Chandigarh or Kullu.

TRAVEL TIPS:
- Paragliding season peaks in Oct–Nov when thermals are
  strongest and skies clearest. Book pilots 1–2 days ahead
  in peak season.
- GHNP entry requires a permit (₹50 Indian, ₹200 foreign)
  at the Sai Ropa entry gate — bring ID.
- Fishing permit for Tirthan river available from the
  Fisheries Office in Banjar (₹200/day). Fly fishing only
  in the upper reaches.
- No large vehicles on the Tirthan Valley road beyond
  Gushaini — only small SUVs or bikes.
- Mobile network drops after Gushaini; inform people
  before entering the valley.

MUST-TRY FOOD:
Freshly grilled Tirthan river trout, Wild honey on
fresh roti, Pahadi dal with ghee, Siddu with mutton,
Tibetan butter tea and tsampa cookies (Bir colony).
